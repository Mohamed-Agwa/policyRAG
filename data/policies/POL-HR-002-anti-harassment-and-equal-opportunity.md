# Anti-Harassment and Equal Opportunity Policy

## 1. Policy Statement

Nile Byte Technologies is committed to a workplace where every person is treated with dignity and respect, and where employment decisions are based on merit. Harassment, sexual harassment, bullying, discrimination and victimisation are prohibited. This policy explains what these behaviours are, how to raise a complaint, how complaints are investigated and what support is available.

This policy supports the Code of Conduct (POL-HR-001) and applies to the same people and situations: employees, interns, contractors, consultants and visitors, at the office, at client sites, while travelling, at Company events, in remote meetings and in online channels such as chat, email and social media.

## 2. Equal Opportunity

Nile Byte makes decisions about recruitment, pay, promotion, training, assignments, discipline and termination based on skills, performance and business needs. We do not discriminate on the basis of sex, religion, belief, ethnic or national origin, colour, age, disability, marital or family status, pregnancy, or any other characteristic protected by law.

### 2.1 Recruitment

- Job advertisements describe the role's requirements and do not state preferences for gender, age, religion or appearance unless there is a genuine and lawful occupational requirement.
- Every technical interview panel for a permanent role includes at least two interviewers, and People Operations aims for gender diversity on every panel.
- Candidates are assessed against the same structured criteria for the same role.

### 2.2 Pay Equity

People Operations reviews pay by role, level and gender every year as part of the salary review described in the Performance Review Policy (POL-HR-007). Unexplained gaps of more than 5% between comparable employees are investigated and corrected.

### 2.3 Reasonable Accommodations

Nile Byte provides reasonable accommodations to employees and candidates with disabilities, health conditions, pregnancy-related needs or religious practices, unless the accommodation would cause disproportionate hardship. Examples include adjusted working hours, assistive software, an ergonomic workstation, accessible interview arrangements and a quiet room for prayer. Requests should be sent to People Operations, who will respond within **10 working days**. Medical details are kept confidential and shared only with those who need them to arrange the accommodation.

## 3. Prohibited Behaviour

### 3.1 Harassment

Harassment is unwanted conduct related to a person's protected characteristic that violates their dignity or creates an intimidating, hostile, degrading, humiliating or offensive environment. It can be a single serious incident or a pattern of behaviour, and it does not matter whether the person intended to cause offence. Examples include:

- jokes, nicknames or comments about a person's religion, origin, gender, age or disability;
- displaying or sharing offensive images, memes or messages;
- mocking accents or imitating disabilities; and
- deliberately excluding someone from meetings or social events because of who they are.

### 3.2 Sexual Harassment

Sexual harassment is any unwanted conduct of a sexual nature, or unwanted conduct related to sex, that violates a person's dignity. It includes:

- unwanted physical contact, including touching, hugging or brushing against someone;
- sexual comments, jokes or questions about a person's private life or body;
- repeated requests for dates after the person has said no;
- sending sexual messages, images or links, including through personal phones or social media;
- staring, leering or gestures of a sexual nature; and
- suggesting that job benefits, assignments or a positive review depend on accepting sexual advances.

Sexual harassment is also a criminal offence under Egyptian law. The Company will support employees who choose to report such conduct to the authorities.

### 3.3 Bullying

Bullying is offensive, intimidating, malicious or insulting behaviour, or an abuse of power, that undermines or humiliates a person. Examples are public criticism designed to humiliate, setting impossible deadlines on purpose, spreading rumours, and persistent undermining of someone's work. Reasonable management actions, such as giving clear feedback, setting performance expectations or starting a performance improvement plan, are not bullying when carried out fairly and respectfully.

### 3.4 Victimisation and Retaliation

Victimisation means treating someone badly because they made a complaint, supported a colleague's complaint or gave evidence in an investigation. Victimisation is treated as seriously as the original misconduct.

## 4. Responsibilities

- **All employees** must treat others with respect, avoid prohibited behaviour and report harassment they witness.
- **Managers** must set an example, act on concerns raised with them within 2 working days, and refer every complaint to People Operations. A manager who ignores a complaint may face disciplinary action.
- **People Operations** manages the complaint process, appoints investigators, provides training and monitors trends.
- **Senior leadership** is accountable for the culture of the Company and receives an anonymised summary of complaints every quarter.

## 5. Raising a Concern

### 5.1 Informal Resolution

Some concerns can be resolved quickly and informally, for example by telling the person that their behaviour is unwelcome and asking them to stop. Employees may ask their manager or a People Operations partner to help with an informal conversation. Informal resolution is always optional: nobody is required to confront the person who harassed them, and serious incidents, particularly sexual harassment or physical misconduct, should go straight to a formal complaint.

### 5.2 Formal Complaint

A formal complaint can be made through any of these channels:

- the employee's manager, or the next manager up if the concern involves the manager;
- People Operations, in person or at people@nilebyte.example;
- the Ethics Hotline (0800-NILE-ETH or online), which accepts anonymous reports.

A complaint should be raised as soon as possible, ideally within **3 months** of the incident, so that evidence and memories are fresh. Complaints made later will still be considered.

The complaint should describe what happened, when and where, who was involved, any witnesses, and any messages or other evidence. Employees may bring a colleague of their choice to any meeting about their complaint.

## 6. Investigation Process

1. **Acknowledgement.** People Operations acknowledges a formal complaint in writing within **2 working days**.
2. **Interim measures.** Where needed, the Company takes immediate steps to protect the people involved, such as changing seating, reporting lines or project assignments, or arranging temporary remote work. Interim measures are not a finding of guilt, and they should not disadvantage the person who complained.
3. **Investigation start.** An impartial investigator, who has had no involvement in the matter, is appointed and begins the investigation within **5 working days** of the complaint.
4. **Interviews and evidence.** The investigator meets the complainant, the person accused and relevant witnesses, and reviews documents and messages. The person accused is told the details of the allegations and has a full opportunity to respond.
5. **Conclusion.** Investigations are normally completed within **30 calendar days**. If more time is needed, both parties are informed of the reason and the new expected date.
6. **Outcome.** Both parties are told in writing whether the complaint was upheld. Details of disciplinary measures against another employee are confidential, but the complainant is told whether action was taken.

### 6.1 Outcomes and Sanctions

If a complaint is upheld, sanctions depend on the seriousness of the behaviour and may include mandatory training, a written warning, a change of role or reporting line, a final written warning or dismissal. Sexual harassment involving physical contact or threats will normally lead to dismissal.

### 6.2 Appeals

Either party may appeal the outcome within **10 working days** of receiving it by writing to the Head of People Operations. The appeal is heard by a senior manager who was not involved in the original decision, and the appeal decision is final within the Company's internal process.

### 6.3 Confidentiality

All parties and witnesses must keep complaint details confidential. Information is shared only with those who need it to investigate or to take action. Records are stored securely by People Operations and retained for 5 years after the case is closed.

## 7. Support

Employees affected by harassment, whether as complainants, witnesses or accused persons, can use the confidential Employee Assistance Programme described in the Benefits and Wellbeing Policy (POL-HR-008). People Operations can also arrange time off, flexible working or a change of team where appropriate.

## 8. Training

All employees complete anti-harassment training within **30 days** of joining and refresher training **every two years**. Managers complete an additional session on handling complaints and preventing retaliation every year.

## 9. Monitoring and Review

People Operations records the number, type and outcome of complaints and reviews the data each quarter to identify patterns. This policy is reviewed every year, or earlier if the law changes.
