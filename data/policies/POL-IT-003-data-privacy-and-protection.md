# Data Privacy and Protection Policy

## 1. Purpose

Nile Byte Technologies processes personal data about employees, candidates, client users and business contacts. This policy explains how the Company collects, uses, stores, shares and deletes personal data responsibly and in line with applicable data protection law, including Egypt's Personal Data Protection Law (Law No. 151 of 2020) and, for European clients, the EU General Data Protection Regulation.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all employees, contractors and interns, and to all personal data processed by or on behalf of Nile Byte in any format, including databases, documents, emails, chat messages, recordings and paper files. It covers personal data processed for the Company's own purposes and personal data processed on behalf of clients.

## 3. Key Definitions

- **Personal data:** any information relating to an identified or identifiable person, such as a name, ID number, email address, phone number, location data, IP address or photo.
- **Sensitive personal data:** personal data revealing health, religion, political opinions, biometric or genetic data, criminal records, or financial information. It requires additional protection.
- **Processing:** any operation on personal data, including collecting, storing, viewing, changing, sharing and deleting it.
- **Data subject:** the person whom the personal data is about.
- **Controller:** the organisation that decides why and how personal data is processed. Nile Byte is the controller for employee and candidate data.
- **Processor:** an organisation that processes personal data on behalf of a controller. Nile Byte is usually a processor when building and operating systems for clients.

## 4. Data Protection Principles

Everyone at Nile Byte must follow these principles when handling personal data:

1. **Lawful and transparent:** process personal data only with a valid legal basis and tell people how their data is used.
2. **Purpose limitation:** use personal data only for the specific purpose for which it was collected.
3. **Data minimisation:** collect only the personal data that is necessary.
4. **Accuracy:** keep personal data accurate and up to date.
5. **Storage limitation:** keep personal data only as long as needed, following the retention schedule in Section 8.
6. **Security:** protect personal data with appropriate measures, as required by the Information Security Policy (POL-IT-001), where personal data is classified as **Restricted**.
7. **Accountability:** be able to show how these principles are met.

## 5. The Data Protection Officer

Nile Byte has appointed a **Data Protection Officer (DPO)**, who can be contacted at **dpo@nilebyte.example**. The DPO advises on data protection, maintains the record of processing activities, reviews new projects that use personal data, handles data subject requests and is the contact point for regulators.

## 6. Privacy by Design

Any new system, product feature, vendor or internal process that involves personal data must consider privacy from the start. Teams must complete a **privacy review** with the DPO before launch. For high-risk processing, such as large-scale processing of sensitive personal data, location tracking or automated decisions about people, a full **Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA)** is required.

Engineering teams must use anonymised or synthetic data in development and test environments, as required by the Information Security Policy (POL-IT-001).

## 7. Client Personal Data

When Nile Byte processes personal data on behalf of clients:

- the data may be used **only** according to the client's documented instructions and the data processing agreement in the contract;
- access is restricted to team members working on the client's project, and is reviewed every quarter;
- client personal data must not be copied to personal devices, unapproved tools or development environments; and
- sub-processors, such as cloud hosting providers, may be used only if the client has approved them.

## 8. Retention and Deletion

Personal data must be deleted or anonymised when it is no longer needed. The standard retention periods are:

| Data category | Retention period |
| --- | --- |
| Unsuccessful candidate applications | 1 year after the recruitment decision, unless the candidate agrees to a longer period |
| Employee personnel records | Duration of employment plus 5 years |
| Payroll and tax records | 10 years, as required by tax law |
| Expense claims and receipts | 7 years |
| Security logs | 12 months |
| Office CCTV recordings | 30 days, unless needed for an investigation |
| Client personal data | As defined in the client contract; deleted or returned within 30 days of contract end |

Data owners must review stored personal data at least once a year and delete data that has passed its retention period.

## 9. Sharing and International Transfers

- Personal data may be shared with third parties only when there is a legal basis and a written contract containing data protection terms.
- Transferring personal data outside Egypt requires approval from the DPO, who checks that the destination provides adequate protection and that any required licence or permit has been obtained.
- Personal data must never be sold.

## 10. Data Subject Rights

Data subjects have rights over their personal data, including the right to access it, correct it, request its deletion, object to certain processing and be informed about how it is used. Any request from an individual about their personal data, whether from an employee, candidate or member of the public, must be forwarded to the DPO **within 2 working days** of receipt. The DPO aims to respond to requests within **30 calendar days**.

If the request relates to client personal data, the DPO informs the client, who as controller decides how to respond.

Employees can view and update much of their own personal data directly in PeopleHub.

## 11. Personal Data Breaches

A personal data breach is any security incident that leads to personal data being lost, destroyed, altered, disclosed or accessed without authorisation. Examples include sending a spreadsheet of client users to the wrong email address, losing an unencrypted USB drive, or a database being accessed by an attacker.

- Any suspected personal data breach must be reported **immediately, and at the latest within 1 hour**, to Information Security (security@nilebyte.example or extension 4444), as required by the Information Security Policy (POL-IT-001). Information Security notifies the DPO.
- The DPO assesses the breach and, where required by law, notifies the data protection regulator **within 72 hours** of the Company becoming aware of it.
- If the breach affects client data, the client is notified **within 24 hours** of the Company becoming aware of it, unless the contract specifies a shorter period.
- The DPO decides whether affected individuals must be informed.

## 12. Employee Personal Data

Nile Byte processes employee personal data to manage employment, pay salaries, provide benefits, meet legal obligations and keep the workplace secure. Employee data is accessible only to People Operations, Finance, managers and IT staff who need it for their role. Salary and medical information is classified as Restricted. Employee monitoring is described in the Acceptable Use of IT Resources Policy (POL-IT-002).

## 13. Training and Compliance

All employees complete data privacy training within **30 days of joining** and every year after that. Breaches of this policy may lead to disciplinary action under the Code of Conduct (POL-HR-001) and may also expose individuals to penalties under data protection law. Questions should be sent to the DPO at dpo@nilebyte.example.
