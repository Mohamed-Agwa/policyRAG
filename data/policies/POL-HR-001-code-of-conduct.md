# Code of Conduct

## 1. Purpose

This Code of Conduct sets out the standards of behaviour that Nile Byte Technologies S.A.E. ("Nile Byte", "the Company") expects from everyone who works for or on behalf of the Company. It explains how we treat one another, our customers, our partners and the communities where we operate. The Code is the foundation for all other Company policies; where another policy gives more detailed rules on a topic, that policy applies together with this Code.

Nile Byte is a software engineering company of roughly 300 people with its headquarters in Smart Village, Giza, and a second office in Alexandria. We build cloud platforms and mobile applications for clients in Egypt, the Gulf region and Europe. Our reputation depends on the integrity of every individual decision made by our employees.

## 2. Scope

This Code applies to:

- all employees, whether permanent, fixed-term, part-time or on probation;
- interns and trainees;
- contractors and consultants working through the Company's systems or on Company premises; and
- members of the Board of Directors when acting on Company business.

The Code applies during working hours, at Company events, while travelling on business, when working remotely, and online whenever a person can reasonably be identified as representing Nile Byte.

## 3. Our Values

Our four values guide how we work:

1. **Integrity first.** We are honest, we keep our promises and we do the right thing even when nobody is watching.
2. **Respect for people.** We treat colleagues, customers and partners with dignity, courtesy and fairness.
3. **Craftsmanship.** We take pride in secure, reliable and well-tested work, and we raise problems early.
4. **Shared ownership.** We take responsibility for outcomes, share knowledge openly and help one another succeed.

## 4. Respectful Workplace

Every person at Nile Byte has the right to work in an environment free from harassment, bullying, discrimination and intimidation. Disrespectful behaviour, including shouting, insulting language, offensive jokes, deliberate exclusion and sharing offensive material, is not acceptable in person, in chat channels, in email or in video meetings.

Detailed rules, definitions and the complaint process are set out in the Anti-Harassment and Equal Opportunity Policy (POL-HR-002).

### 4.1 Dress and Appearance

The Company dress code is business casual. Employees meeting clients in person, visiting client sites or representing the Company at external events are expected to dress in a manner appropriate to the client's environment. Clothing must not display offensive slogans or images. Employees may wear religious dress and symbols at all times.

### 4.2 Alcohol, Drugs and Smoking

Employees must not work under the influence of alcohol or illegal drugs. Possession or use of illegal drugs on Company premises or at Company events is grounds for dismissal. Smoking, including electronic cigarettes, is permitted only in the designated outdoor smoking areas at each office.

## 5. Conflicts of Interest

A conflict of interest arises when a personal interest could influence, or appear to influence, an employee's judgement on behalf of the Company. Examples include:

- holding a financial interest in a supplier, customer or competitor;
- hiring, supervising or approving the pay of a close relative or partner;
- working for, advising or owning a business that competes with Nile Byte; and
- using Company information to make personal investments.

Employees must disclose any actual or potential conflict of interest in writing to their manager and to People Operations within **14 calendar days** of becoming aware of it, using the Conflict of Interest Declaration form in PeopleHub. Disclosure does not mean the employee has done anything wrong; it allows the Company to manage the situation, for example by changing approval responsibilities.

### 5.1 Outside Employment

Employees may take on outside work, freelance projects or paid board positions only with prior written approval from their manager and People Operations. Approval will normally be refused if the outside work competes with Nile Byte, serves a Nile Byte client or competitor, uses Company equipment or information, or affects the employee's performance. Unpaid volunteer and charitable work does not require approval unless it involves a Company client.

### 5.2 Relationships at Work

Personal relationships between colleagues are a private matter. However, an employee may not directly or indirectly supervise, evaluate or approve the pay of a person with whom they have a close personal relationship. Such relationships must be disclosed to People Operations so that reporting lines can be adjusted. Disclosures are handled confidentially.

## 6. Gifts, Hospitality and Anti-Bribery

Nile Byte has zero tolerance for bribery and corruption. Employees must never offer, promise, give, request or accept anything of value to improperly influence a business decision. This applies to dealings with private companies and government officials alike.

### 6.1 Gifts

- Employees may accept occasional gifts of modest value from business contacts, up to **EGP 1,000** per gift and up to **EGP 3,000** in total from the same source per calendar year.
- Any gift above these limits must be declared in the Gift Register in PeopleHub within 5 working days and will normally be returned or donated.
- Cash, gift cards, vouchers and other cash equivalents must never be accepted, whatever the amount.
- Gifts must never be accepted during an active tender, contract negotiation or procurement decision involving the giver.

### 6.2 Hospitality

Reasonable business meals and event invitations are acceptable if they have a clear business purpose, are not lavish, and are not frequent. Invitations that include travel or overnight accommodation paid by a supplier or client require prior approval from a director. Rules for spending Company money on client entertainment are in the Travel and Expense Reimbursement Policy (POL-FIN-001).

### 6.3 Facilitation Payments

Facilitation payments (small unofficial payments to speed up routine government actions) are prohibited. If an employee is asked for such a payment, they must refuse and report the request to the Legal team the same day. The only exception is a payment made under immediate threat to personal safety, which must be reported as soon as the employee is safe.

## 7. Protecting Company Assets and Information

Company assets include laptops, phones, software licences, office equipment, funds, intellectual property and information. Employees must use Company assets responsibly, protect them from loss, theft and misuse, and use them primarily for business purposes. Limited personal use of IT resources is described in the Acceptable Use of IT Resources Policy (POL-IT-002).

### 7.1 Confidential Information

Employees must keep confidential all non-public information about Nile Byte, its clients and its partners, including source code, product plans, pricing, client data and employee data. Confidentiality obligations continue after employment ends. Information must be handled according to its classification under the Information Security Policy (POL-IT-001), and personal data must be handled according to the Data Privacy and Protection Policy (POL-IT-003).

### 7.2 Intellectual Property

Software, designs, documentation and inventions created by employees in the course of their work belong to Nile Byte or to the client, as defined in the relevant contract. Employees must not use open-source components in client deliverables without following the Engineering open-source approval process, and must never copy code or content that the Company does not have the right to use.

## 8. Communication and Public Statements

Only the Chief Executive Officer, the Marketing Director and people they authorise may speak to journalists or make public statements on behalf of Nile Byte. Media enquiries must be forwarded to marketing@nilebyte.example. When employees discuss their work on social media or at conferences, they must make clear that the views are their own, and must not disclose confidential information or client names without permission.

## 9. Fair Dealing

We compete fairly. Employees must not make false statements about competitors, obtain competitor information by improper means, or agree with competitors on prices, salaries or the division of clients. We select suppliers on merit, price and quality, and we treat them honestly.

## 10. Health and Safety

Everyone is responsible for keeping the workplace safe. Employees must follow office safety instructions, take part in fire evacuation drills, keep walkways clear and report hazards, accidents and near misses to the Office Manager on the same day. Employees working remotely should follow the home workspace guidance in the Remote and Hybrid Work Policy (POL-HR-005).

## 11. Speaking Up

Employees who see or suspect a breach of this Code, of any Company policy or of the law are expected to speak up. Concerns can be raised through any of the following channels:

- the employee's line manager;
- People Operations at people@nilebyte.example;
- the Legal team at legal@nilebyte.example; or
- the independent **Ethics Hotline**, available 24 hours a day by phone at 0800-NILE-ETH or online, where reports can be made anonymously.

Reports are treated confidentially and shared only with the people who need to know in order to investigate.

### 11.1 Non-Retaliation

Nile Byte strictly prohibits retaliation against anyone who raises a concern in good faith or who cooperates with an investigation. Retaliation includes dismissal, demotion, exclusion from projects, negative reviews or harassment linked to a report. Anyone who retaliates will face disciplinary action, up to and including dismissal. Knowingly making a false report is itself a breach of this Code.

## 12. Breaches and Disciplinary Action

Breaches of this Code are investigated by People Operations, with support from Legal where needed. Depending on the seriousness of the breach, consequences may include a verbal warning, a written warning, a final written warning, suspension or dismissal, in line with Egyptian labour law and the Company's disciplinary procedure. Serious breaches such as fraud, bribery, theft, violence or deliberate disclosure of client data may lead to immediate dismissal and may be reported to the authorities.

## 13. Annual Acknowledgement

All employees must read this Code and confirm in PeopleHub that they understand it when they join and again every year by **31 January**. Managers are responsible for making sure that their team members complete the acknowledgement and for leading by example.

## 14. Questions and Review

Questions about this Code should be directed to People Operations. This Code is reviewed at least once a year by People Operations and Legal and approved by the Chief Executive Officer.
